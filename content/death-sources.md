- **Horace**, *Odes* 1.4 (23 BC): pale Death knocks with an impartial foot at the cottages of the poor and the palaces of kings. [Text](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_works_of_Horace/First_Book_of_Odes)
- **Marcus Aurelius**, *Meditations* 6.24: death brought Alexander the Great and his mule driver to the same state. [Text](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Marcus_Aurelius_(Haines_1916)/Book_6)
- **Seneca**, *On the Shortness of Life*: life isn't short, we waste much of it, mostly because we forget it's limited. [Summary](https://dailystoic.com/on-the-shortness-of-life-seneca/)
- **Michel de Montaigne**, "That to Study Philosophy Is to Learn to Die" (1580): to premeditate death is to premeditate freedom. [Text](https://publicdomainreview.org/collection/that-to-study-philosophy-is-to-learn-to-die-1580)
- **Hávamál**, verse 76 (Old Norse, Viking Age): cattle die, kin die, you die the same; a good name never dies. Sung in Wardruna's *Helvegen*.
