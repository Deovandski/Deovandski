## Why it's in Philosophy

Cycling lives here because it's about health: pushing myself to go outside more and touch grass. It was a good fit for the days when the weather was too nice to spend in the gym, especially during the stretch when the gym was a stress relief tool rather than something I truly enjoyed.

Having cycling in the routine, alongside the gym, also helped my circadian rhythm and my sleep quality. Sleep is a known problem for AuDHD people, and the research lines up with what I felt:

- Sleep problems are far more common in autistic people and in people with ADHD than in their neurotypical peers, insomnia and a body clock that runs late above all, and having both tends to make it worse.
- Daylight, especially in the morning, is what sets the body clock. More morning sun goes with an earlier, steadier sleep.
- Regular exercise brings moderate gains in sleep quality and helps you fall asleep faster.

A ride outside stacks the last two: daylight and exercise, in one go. The studies are in this tab's Links box.

How my nights after exercise, rides included, compare with rest days is in the [Biomarker Lab](#biomarkers-exercise), under Hearth.

It's also here for the same reason as the [Gym](#gym). AuDHD people tend to get stuck inside their own homes and fall behind socially: home is safe, quiet and predictable, and the world outside costs energy. The research lines up with that too:

- Autistic people report far more loneliness than neurotypical people, and that loneliness goes hand in hand with anxiety and depression.
- Across 82 studies, autistic and ADHD men were as socially connected as other men at best, and never more. Less connection came with worse mental health.

Just like the gym, cycling is a way for my ADHD to challenge the autistic side and bring balance to the order of things. The autistic side wants the same safe routine at home; the ADHD side wants something new. A ride gives both: a routine I can count on, with a new trail or a new hill every time. The studies point the same way:

- Exercise sharpens inhibitory control (the brakes on impulses) in adults with ADHD, after a single session and even more with regular training.
- Exercise programs improve social communication in autistic people (most of the studies are on kids), above all the ones done in groups and kept up for longer.

And it's my [Mentor](#mentor) tab, applied to myself: **comfort may visit me, but it will never become my god.** Movement creates evidence, and I'm not dying in the waiting room of my own stagnant life either.

## Mountain biking

Going mountain biking for the first time was a natural step up to expand my experiences, although my bike did not survive the endeavor.

I plan to explore more mountain biking, but nothing of the hard or extreme routes: I value still having my teeth, and I was not born with high dexterity (as you can clearly see in my [paintings](#painting)).
