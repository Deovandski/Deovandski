## Hard truths

- Movement creates evidence. Refuse limbo: prefer an honest no to a manipulative maybe. Feelings are telemetry. **When intuition and evidence point the same way, ignoring them is just fear dressed up as logic.** Move.
- Learn hard things and enter rooms bigger than your competence. Pain isn't proof of growth, and time doesn't heal you; changed behavior is the only proof. **Comfort may visit me. It will never become my god.**
- Dream violently. Plan soberly. Execute relentlessly. Update honestly. **Unapplied lessons and unexecuted intentions are the same thing: masturbatory self-entertainment.**
- People can open doors, but nobody walks through them using your legs. Potential earns encouragement, not applause. **Sustained growth is what earns the praise.**
- Confidence is not noise. **It's trusting yourself to do what's right when your core is shaken.** Build boldly, measure honestly, correct without ego.
- **Say what you mean. Do what you say. If your direction changes, own it.** Don't control me, challenge me. Don't flatter me, engage me. Don't promise me, show me.
- **Peace is not passivity.** A boundary without enforcement is a request. Compassion can explain behavior without absorbing it, and standing idle while someone self-destructs is idiot compassion, not peace. Say your piece, then walk away.
- Access is earned through behavior, never granted because someone asked. Time, attention and trust are finite; my attention is not public property. **I will meet you in the middle. I will not live there alone.**
- **Full commitment is not eternal commitment.** If there's movement, I work with imperfection; if the system is stuck in limbo, I stop feeding it. When people prove unworthy of your love, losing faith isn't failure; it's clarity. I steward living systems and bury dead ones.
- Engineer and artist. Founder and teammate. Traveler and home-builder. Intense builder and quiet cat dad. **I refuse to amputate parts of myself to become easier to classify.**

<details class="sources">
<summary>Shoulders I stand on</summary>

- **Aristotle**, *Nicomachean Ethics*, Book II: we become just by doing just acts. Character is built by repeated action. [Text](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.2.ii.html)
- **Theodore Roosevelt**, "The Strenuous Life," Hamilton Club, Chicago, April 10, 1899: "not the doctrine of ignoble ease, but the doctrine of the strenuous life." The speech was also an argument for American empire; what I take from it is the personal part: choose worthy difficulty. [Text](https://voicesofdemocracy.umd.edu/?p=927)
- **Edward Deci & Richard Ryan**, Self-Determination Theory: autonomy, competence and relatedness as basic psychological needs. [Overview](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/theory/)
- **Steven C. Hayes**, Acceptance and Commitment Therapy's psychological flexibility: stay present with what you feel, and persist or change in service of chosen values. [ACBS](https://contextualscience.org/the_six_core_processes_of_act)
- **Peter Gollwitzer**, implementation intentions ("if X happens, then I will do Y"). His 2006 meta-analysis with Paschal Sheeran, across 94 tests, found a medium-to-large effect on reaching goals. [Paper](https://www.decisionskills.com/uploads/5/1/6/0/5160560/2006_gollwitzersheeran_implementation_intentions.pdf)
- **Gabriele Oettingen**, mental contrasting and WOOP: wish, outcome, obstacle, plan. [WOOP](https://woopmylife.org/)
- **K. Anders Ericsson, Ralf Krampe & Clemens Tesch-Römer** (1993), deliberate practice. Honest update: Brooke Macnamara and Megha Maitra's 2019 replication found practice matters, but much less than the original study claimed. [Replication](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6731745/)
- **Carsten Wrosch and colleagues** (2003): letting go of unattainable goals and reengaging with new ones is linked to higher well-being. [Paper](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/psychology/pdf/scales/GAS_article.pdf)
- **John Welwood**, Buddhist teacher and psychotherapist, coined "spiritual bypassing" in 1984: using spiritual ideas and practices to sidestep unresolved emotional issues and unfinished work. [Interview](http://www.johnwelwood.com/articles/TRIC_interview_uncut.pdf)
- **Chögyam Trungpa**, Tibetan Buddhist teacher, coined "idiot compassion": kindness that avoids conflict. His student Pema Chödrön describes it as enabling. [Pema Chödrön](https://tricycle.org/?p=1701)
- **Erik Erikson**, generativity: caring for what will outlive you. Its virtue is care. [Reference](https://link.springer.com/referenceworkentry/10.1007/978-0-387-79061-9_1218)
- **John Wooden**, Pyramid of Success: poise and confidence sit near the top, built on every block beneath; competitive greatness is the peak. [Pyramid](https://coachwooden.com/pyramid-of-success)
