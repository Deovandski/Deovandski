- **Ren Riley**, *Wavy Thoughts* (2024), "I get tattoos to remind me that my body exists": an AuDHD writer on tattoos as a way to get to know a body they had long felt disconnected from, and on the needle as a grounding practice. [Article](https://buttondown.com/RenRiley/archive/i-get-tattoos-to-remind-me-that-my/)
- **Lynn Clemens**, *Mediamatic*, "The Links between Tattoo Culture and Autism": an autistic artist on getting and giving tattoos as a kind of stimming, a steady, predictable input in a loud world. [Article](https://www.mediamatic.net/en/id/387277)
- **Andy Clark & David Chalmers**, *Analysis* (1998), "The Extended Mind": Otto's notebook does the job of his memory, so where it lives matters less than what it does. Skin, a whiteboard or a website can hold part of the mind too. [Paper](https://web.ics.purdue.edu/~drkelly/ClarkChalmersTheExtendedMind1998.pdf)
- ***Attention* magazine (CHADD)** (2025), "Remembering the Future": with ADHD, lean on outside reminders instead of "I'll remember", and make them impossible to miss and meaningful to you. A word on each wrist qualifies. [Article](https://chadd.org/attention-article/remembering-the-future-how-adhd-affects-prospective-memory-and-how-to-work-with-it/)
- **Kristina Klug, Christin Camia & Sonja Rohrmann**, *Memory, Mind & Media* (2024), "Tattoos embody autobiographical memories": in 161 tattooed adults, tattoos worked as memory objects, and the ones not tied to an event still carried mottos, beliefs and values. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1017/mem.2024.6)
- **Francois Naudé & Luzelle Naudé** (2024), "(Tat)who Am I? Tattoos as an Expression of Narrative Identity": people use tattoos to explore and tell the story of who they are. [Summary in *Psychology Today*](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/head-games/202403/5-ways-your-tattoos-reveal-who-you-are)
- **Matthew D. Lieberman and colleagues**, *Psychological Science* (2007), "Putting Feelings Into Words": naming a feeling calmed the brain's alarm (the amygdala). Writing it all down on a site is a long way of doing the same. [Abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17576282/)
- **Eilidh Cage & Zoe Troxell-Whitman**, *Autism in Adulthood* (2020): autistic adults with a stronger autistic identity were more openly autistic, and being open went with less camouflaging (masking). [Paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8992868/)
